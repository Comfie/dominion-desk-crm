# DominionDesk Product Landing Page Design

## Goal

Rework the public landing page into a product page for DominionDesk as a South African rental operations platform. The page must move the product beyond "a system" and position it as a launch-ready SaaS product for three account types:

- Private landlords
- Property companies
- Rental agents and agencies

The page must surface the new agency placement journey without making private landlords feel secondary. It must include the meaningful implemented features while avoiding fake testimonials, unsupported metrics, and claims around integrations or payments that are still placeholders.

## Product Positioning

Primary positioning:

> DominionDesk is the rental operations OS for South Africa, covering the lifecycle from mandate and application through tenant placement, rent collection, maintenance, documents, and reporting.

Hero headline:

> Run the rental lifecycle from mandate to monthly rent.

Hero subtext:

> DominionDesk brings placement, tenant management, rent collection, maintenance, documents, and reporting into one South African property operations platform for landlords, property companies, and rental agents.

Primary CTA:

> Start free

Secondary CTA:

> See product walkthrough

## Page Structure

### 1. Hero: Product Thesis

The hero must communicate the whole product, not only landlord admin.

Content:

- Headline: "Run the rental lifecycle from mandate to monthly rent."
- Subheadline: product positioning above.
- CTA buttons: "Start free" and "See product walkthrough".
- Small trust row: "Built for South Africa", "Private, company, and agency accounts", "Tenant portal included".

Visual:

- A constructed product surface, not a decorative abstract hero.
- Three lane cards inside the product surface:
  - Placement
  - Management
  - Tenant Portal
- Each lane shows realistic statuses, for example:
  - Placement: "Mandate active", "Viewing scheduled", "Screening passed"
  - Management: "Rent due", "Maintenance open", "Report exported"
  - Tenant Portal: "Invoice viewed", "POP uploaded", "Issue logged"

### 2. Audience Product Paths

Three cards for private landlords, property companies, and rental agents. Each card should include:

- One clear promise
- 6-8 feature bullets
- A product-relevant icon
- CTA text such as "Explore landlord tools", "Explore portfolio tools", or "Explore placement tools"

These can scroll to the relevant page sections rather than creating separate pages.

### 3. Lifecycle Rail

This is the signature section. It should give the page a specific identity that generic SaaS pages do not have.

Rail:

1. Mandate
2. Application
3. Viewing
4. Screening
5. Placement
6. Lease
7. Rent
8. Maintenance
9. Reports

Layout:

- Desktop: horizontal operational rail with connected status blocks.
- Mobile: vertical timeline.
- Each step gets a short label and one sentence explaining what DominionDesk controls at that stage.

Purpose:

- Show that the product covers both pre-lease placement and post-lease management.
- Make the agent placement work visible immediately.

### 4. Placement Suite

Audience: rental agents and agencies.

Features to include:

- Agency-only placement workspace
- Landlord owner register
- Mandate register
- Placement-only and management mandate support
- Placement fee and monthly management fee tracking
- Application intake
- Inquiry-to-application support where available
- Viewing scheduling
- Viewing attendance statuses
- Applicant screening
- Credit, affordability, employer, landlord reference, FICA, and consent checks
- Placement completion
- Tenant resolution by linked tenant, email match, or new tenant
- Lease/property assignment
- Tenant portal activation handoff

Design:

- Use a compact "placement board" visual with columns:
  - Mandates
  - Applications
  - Viewings
  - Screening
  - Placed
- Avoid large decorative cards. This should feel operational and agent-ready.

### 5. Property Management Suite

Audience: private landlords and property companies.

Features to include:

- Property creation and editing
- Property images
- Property documents
- Long-term, short-term, and mixed rental types
- Monthly rent, daily rate, cleaning fee, and security deposit fields
- Property import
- Property status tracking
- Multi-tenant per property support
- Unit labels for rooms or units
- Tenant profiles
- Emergency contact information
- Lease assignments
- Tenant status management
- Tenant document storage
- Maintenance requests
- Maintenance photos through tenant portal
- Inspections
- Tasks
- Notifications

Design:

- Use a two-column product breakdown:
  - "Manage the asset"
  - "Manage the relationship"
- Show small status chips and table-like rows, not generic illustration blocks.

### 6. Tenant Portal

Audience: landlords, companies, agents, and tenants.

Features to include:

- Tenant portal login
- Invoice viewing
- Payment history
- Proof of EFT payment upload
- Maintenance request logging
- Maintenance photo upload
- Tenant documents
- Profile and contact details
- Portal access activation after placement

Positioning:

- Tenant portal should be described as the handoff layer that reduces admin after placement.
- State that tenants get portal access; do not offer a "welcome email without portal access" path.

### 7. Financial Control And Reports

Audience: landlords and portfolio operators.

Features to include:

- Rent collection view
- Payment tracking
- Payment statuses
- Invoice generation
- Banking details
- Automated payment reminders
- Manual payment reminder triggers
- Bulk payment reminders
- Income tracking
- Expense tracking
- Tax-deductible expense flags
- Rent collection report
- Tenant payment ledger
- Revenue report
- Cash flow report
- Occupancy report
- Aging receivables report
- Lease expiration report
- Maintenance costs report
- Tax summary report
- CSV exports

Design:

- Use a financial control panel with a report list and sample rows.
- Avoid unsupported claims such as "guaranteed on-time payments".

### 8. Operations Layer

Audience: companies and agencies.

Features to include:

- Team member management
- Role-based access
- Account type rules: individual, company, agency, tenant
- Agency-only placement navigation
- Messaging templates
- Messaging automation UI
- Scheduled message queue
- Booking event automation triggers
- Payment and maintenance automation triggers
- Task management
- Document vault
- Audit trail
- Notifications

Design:

- Use a compact systems layer section that shows how workflows are governed.
- Copy should focus on control, repeatability, and visibility.

### 9. Booking And Inquiry Workflows

Audience: short-term rental operators and mixed portfolios.

Features to include:

- Booking creation and management
- Guest details
- Booking status workflow
- Availability and conflict checking
- Pricing calculation
- Cleaning fees
- Booking calendar
- Inquiry management
- Inquiry-to-booking support where available
- Booking reports

Important honesty note:

- Do not claim live Airbnb, Booking.com, Google Calendar, Paystack, or Stripe sync as production-ready. If mentioned, place under "planned integrations" or omit from the main product promise.

### 10. South African Trust Section

Content:

- Native ZAR workflows
- EFT-first rent collection
- Proof of payment upload
- POPIA-aware data handling
- PayFast subscription billing
- Local rental terminology and workflows
- Built for landlords, companies, and agents operating in South Africa

Tone:

- Practical and local, not patriotic decoration.
- Avoid unsupported legal claims beyond "POPIA-aware".

### 11. Pricing And Early Access

Current pricing can remain for landlord plans if already accurate, but the page must not invent final agency pricing.

Recommended structure:

- Free trial: keep current 2-month founding member offer if still desired.
- Landlord starter: show existing pricing only if confirmed in app/product.
- Portfolio/company: use existing pricing if confirmed.
- Agency: "Talk to us" or "Agency early access" until pricing is finalized.

Agency pricing copy:

> For agencies managing placement, mandates, and tenant handoff. We will help configure your workspace around your current placement process.

### 12. FAQ

Questions to include:

- Who is DominionDesk for?
- What is the difference between private, company, and agency accounts?
- Can rental agents manage placement before a lease starts?
- Do tenants get portal access?
- How does EFT proof of payment work?
- Can I manage rooms or multiple tenants in one property?
- Does it support short-term rentals?
- What reports can I export?
- Is PayFast billing live?
- Are Airbnb, Booking.com, Google Calendar, Paystack, and Stripe integrations live?
- What happens to my data if I cancel?

## Visual Direction

The page should feel like a product operations surface, not a generic marketing page.

Design tokens:

- Dominion Navy: #08233F
- Signal Blue: #0EA5E9
- Ledger Green: #16A34A
- Mandate Amber: #F59E0B
- Paper White: #F8FAFC
- Ink Slate: #0F172A

Typography:

- Use the existing Geist setup for implementation consistency.
- Make the hero type tighter, more product-led, and less oversized than a generic SaaS splash.
- Use uppercase micro-labels sparingly for operational categories like "PLACEMENT", "MANAGEMENT", and "PORTAL".

Signature design element:

- The rental lifecycle rail.
- It should look like an operational control line with stages and statuses, not a decorative timeline.

Aesthetic risk:

- Replace decorative gradient orbs with a "mandate ledger" visual language: status chips, stamped workflow states, connected rail stages, and subtle table/grid surfaces.

## Copy Principles

- Be direct and specific.
- Sell the product, not vague transformation.
- Do not use fake testimonials or fabricated metrics.
- Do not overpromise unverified integrations.
- Use words South African users recognize: rent, EFT, proof of payment, FICA, mandate, tenant portal, lease, maintenance, portfolio.
- Avoid in-app instructional copy on the public page; use concise product claims.

## Implementation Notes

Primary files likely involved:

- `app/page.tsx`
- `app/components/LandingPageClient.tsx`
- `app/components/Button.tsx` only if existing variants are insufficient
- `app/components/Card.tsx` only if existing card styling blocks the new design

Implementation should prefer:

- Data arrays for product paths, lifecycle stages, feature groups, and FAQ items.
- Focused presentational components inside `LandingPageClient.tsx` or small local components if the file becomes too large.
- Existing logos and mockup assets where useful.
- Constructed product visuals in HTML/CSS where screenshots do not exist.

## Testing And Verification

Minimum verification:

- Landing page renders without runtime errors.
- Navigation anchors work.
- Mobile menu works.
- Audience path cards fit on mobile.
- Lifecycle rail becomes a vertical timeline on mobile.
- Product visuals do not overlap text.
- CTAs link to `/register` and `/demo`.
- No unsupported claims remain in the visible copy.
- Type-check passes.
- Focused lint passes for touched files.
- Build passes if feasible within the session.

## Out Of Scope

- New agency pricing checkout logic.
- New public product subpages per user type.
- Live external integration implementation.
- New testimonial collection.
- Rewriting registration or billing flows.
- Changing the dashboard feature implementation.
